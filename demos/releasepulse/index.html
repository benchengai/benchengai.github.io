ReleasePulse

App-store reviews and issue tickets turned into per-release, per-feature evidence for prioritisation. Rates over de-duplicated volume, a 95% interval on every number, and a quote behind every row.

Research basis

ReleasePulse is grounded in the systematic mapping study Emotions in Software Engineering (57 studies, five emotion categories × five lifecycle phases). Its map has one study in the cell decision-making × requirements engineering (Iqbal et al. 2023, JSS 197), and the decision-making studies it found are almost all post-hoc analyses of maintenance artefacts. This app moves that evidence to the moment a prioritisation decision is made.

  • Feature-level opinion mining of app reviews for requirements work: Guzman & Maalej, “How Do Users Like This Feature? A Fine Grained Sentiment Analysis of App Reviews”, RE 2014. User emotion in app reviews and tweets: Martens & Johann, SEmotion 2017; Williams & Mahmoud, SEmotion 2017 (both in the mapping study).
  • Loudness and recency as availability effects: Tversky & Kahneman, “Availability: A heuristic for judging frequency and probability”, Cognitive Psychology 5 (1973). Cognitive biases in software engineering, with mitigation rarely studied: Mohanani et al., IEEE TSE 46(12), 2020.
  • Intervals: Wilson score interval (Wilson, JASA 22, 1927) for rates; Newcombe’s hybrid score interval for a difference of proportions (Statistics in Medicine 17, 1998).
  • Polarity and complaint or praise cues instead of emotion labels: inferring emotions from expressions is unreliable (Barrett et al., Psychological Science in the Public Interest 20, 2019).
Limitations
  • The sample apps and every review in them are synthetic, written from templates by the same person who wrote the gold labels and the prompts. Real feedback is messier; the scores here are a smoke test, not a benchmark.
  • Rates are shares of feedback items, not of users. People who write reviews are self-selected and lean negative, so a 10% complaint rate does not mean 10% of users are affected.
  • Reviews and issue tickets share one denominator per release. Release assignment by date (for items without a version) is approximate.
  • Duplicate detection is lexical: a paraphrased campaign is counted several times.
  • Clustering can split one feature into several rows (for example “editor” and “checklists”), which spreads its evidence and lowers its rank.
  • Each feature is tested at every release change, at 95% each, with no correction for multiple comparisons; the minimum of 5 complaints limits but does not remove false alarms. Only consecutive releases are compared, so a slow drift is not flagged.
  • Quotes are checked against the item text; polarity and feature assignment are the model’s and are not.
  • Candidate requirements are proposals worded by a model and need confirming with stakeholders.

Privacy: this analyses customer feedback text about a product. It scores no person and infers no emotions about anyone (EU AI Act Art. 5(1)(f) prohibits emotion inference about workers). A file you load here is read in your browser and not uploaded.

Claims here say “grounded in” the research, not “validated”: the evaluation uses synthetic data. Run your own feedback through the command line (releasepulse run your.csv) and load the resulting analysis.json above.
