Method, research basis and limitations

Research basis

  • In the mapping study Emotions in Software Engineering (Cheng, Wang, Arora, Hoang, Liu; 57 studies, classified by emotion category and SDLC phase), requirements engineering is one of the least-studied phases (7 of 57 studies). Communication-and-collaboration research has no requirements-engineering studies at all, although stakeholder interaction peaks there.
  • Every communication study in the map is observational; none evaluates a tool that supports communication as it happens.
  • Decision-making-bias research is almost entirely post hoc (6 of 7 studies mine artefacts after decisions were made). ElicitSense brings the signal to the moment requirement decisions are taken.
  • Requirement changes, especially late ones, are a main trigger of emotional responses (Madampe et al.).
  • Facial configurations are not reliable fingerprints of discrete emotions (Barrett et al. 2019), so ElicitSense reports cues such as “long pause, hedging” rather than labels such as “angry”.
  • It productises MEmoRE, a multimodal requirements-elicitation prototype (Cheng et al., IEEE RE 2023).

How outputs are kept honest

  • The model proposes; code decides. Extraction and linking use strict JSON schemas, and statuses, supersession, conflicts and history are applied deterministically.
  • Every requirement and every Q&A answer cites verbatim quotes. A quote that does not match its transcript segment is rejected and reported, not shown as evidence.
  • Q&A answers marked answerable without a verified citation are shown as unsupported. Questions the meetings never covered should be declined, and are scored.
  • Merge link ids are restricted to the ids in the prompt, so constrained decoding cannot invent one.

Privacy and compliance

  • Audio is transcribed on the analyst’s machine; only transcript text is sent to the model.
  • Cues are attached to requirements, never aggregated per person. There is no per-person score anywhere.
  • Cues are off for internal team meetings (--setting internal): the EU AI Act, Art. 5(1)(f), prohibits AI systems that infer emotions of people in the workplace. The intended setting is consented client elicitation sessions.
  • The demo meetings are fictional and synthetic.

Evaluation design

  • Two gold-labelled projects: harbour_physio is the development set (prompts were written against it); fernhill_bakery was written before any model ran on it and its results did not change the prompts.
  • Each model runs each project three times on identical transcripts; tables report the mean and range, cost from published prices and latency.
  • Q&A is scored on gold questions: required facts, declining never-discussed questions, citation validity and coverage of the gold evidence lines.

Limitations

  • Synthetic audio from speech synthesis, with scripted hesitation. Real meetings have overlapping speech, accents and noise.
  • Two small fictional projects (40 gold requirements, 16 relations): one relation moves recall by 0.11 to 0.14, so differences of that size are noise.
  • One person wrote the prompts, scripts and gold labels. The held-out project limits overfitting to one project, not to one way of describing requirements.
  • Matching is automatic (evidence-line overlap) and was spot-checked, not adjudicated by a second annotator. Q&A facts are matched by keywords, which undercounts paraphrases.
  • Speaker turns come from the synthesis script; a diarization step for real recordings is not built yet.
Demo mode: every requirement, link, answer and score on this site was produced by a real run of the ElicitSense pipeline on synthetic, fictional meetings. Nothing here was written by hand to look like model output.
